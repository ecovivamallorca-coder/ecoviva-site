ETICS installation details that should be checked before rendering

A finished render can look perfectly uniform while the decisive details beneath it remain invisible. These real construction-stage photographs show why façade insulation needs technical follow-up before it is covered.

What these photographs do — and do not — show

These photographs were taken during ongoing façade insulation works in central Palma in May 2026. The contractor and exact location are intentionally not identified.

Because the installation was not yet finished, the images are not an assessment of the completed façade. They do, however, document details that should be checked and, where necessary, corrected before the reinforcement layer and final render conceal them.

The visible finish is only as reliable as the system hidden beneath it.

Why supervision matters before the façade is closed

Property owners are entitled to expect that commissioned work is executed as a complete, technically coherent system—not merely that the finished surface looks neat.

Irregular insulation joints, improvised infill pieces and unresolved connections around windows, doors, penetrations and balcony thresholds can disappear completely behind the render. If nobody checks these stages, potential weak points may remain hidden until cracks, water ingress, thermal bridges or local detachment appear later.

Technical follow-up is not about distrusting every contractor. It is about verifying critical stages while details are still visible and corrections are straightforward, proportionate and relatively inexpensive.

Eight site photographs, four critical control points

The photographs are most useful when read as a sequence: insulation layout, openings, reinforcement and exposed junctions must work together as one continuous system.

01

Service penetrations and interrupted insulation

Pipes, cables and brackets interrupt the insulation layer. The perimeter should be resolved deliberately so the insulation, reinforcement and weather protection remain continuous—not simply hidden with mortar or foam.

02

Windows and doors are high-risk junctions

Openings concentrate movement and water exposure. Returns, frame connections, heads and corners need compatible profiles, continuous insulation and correctly planned reinforcement. Large foam-filled gaps are not a substitute for a complete junction detail.

03

Board layout, joints and reinforcement

Insulation boards should form a stable, continuous layer with controlled joints. Mesh must be fully embedded with the correct overlaps, and openings normally require additional diagonal reinforcement to reduce crack concentration at the corners.

04

Thresholds, balcony edges and splash-water zones

The bottom of the system and transitions to thresholds, balcony floors and railings are exposed to water and impact. These junctions require a planned termination, compatible waterproofing and a continuous route for drainage.

What should be checked before the reinforcement and render are completed?

  • Insulation boards are bonded and fixed according to the selected system
  • Board joints are controlled and unsuitable gaps or loose infill pieces are corrected
  • Window and door reveals, heads, sills and frame connections are continuous
  • Corner profiles, mesh overlaps and diagonal reinforcement are correctly installed
  • Penetrations, brackets, cables and services have durable, watertight details
  • Plinths, thresholds, balcony junctions and splash-water zones are resolved
  • Photographic records are made before the work becomes concealed
